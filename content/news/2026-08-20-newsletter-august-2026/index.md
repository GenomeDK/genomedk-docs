The past year has been another fun and eventful year at GenomeDK. With our
latest investments in CPU and GPU capacity, GenomeDK is now more powerful than
ever.

We now support 1100+ users from all over Denmark (and indeed, other
countries too), and as you can read below, we're soon to welcome even more 
researchers in the future.

<!-- more -->

## The Faculty of Technical Sciences joins GenomeDK collaboration

We're very happy to welcome the Faculty of Technical Sciences (TECH) as the
newest partner in the GenomeDK collaboration!

From 2026, TECH researchers can use GenomeDK on the same conditions as
researchers from NAT, HEALTH and the Central Denmark Region. 

TECH will also have a direct role in the future development of GenomeDK, with
Mogens Sandø Lund from QGG representing the faculty in both the Strategic
Steering Committee and Leader Forum.

The agreement fits very well with AU's new HPC strategy, which calls for
stronger collaboration across faculties, easier access to HPC resources and
better use of shared infrastructure. For us, this is a very concrete step in
that direction -- and we look forward to supporting even more engineering,
simulation, AI and data-intensive research at AU.

[Read more in the announcement](https://genome.au.dk/news/tech-joins-genomedk/)

## New compute resources available

We've [expanded the GenomeDK compute
infrastructure](https://genome.au.dk/news/major-investment-boosts-capacity/)
with additional compute, both CPU and GPU. This means that GenomeDK is now a 
21000+ core cluster!

The new CPU nodes have been integrated into the existing queues, so no changes 
are required to your workflows.

Since we now have multiple types of GPUs available, and more users are making
use of the GPUs, we have updated our documentation for 
[working with GPUs](https://genome.au.dk/docs/computing-with-gpus/). The pricing
page has also been updated accordingly.

If your workload uses MPI or you're curious about how MPI can be used on 
GenomeDK, we've also updated our 
[MPI documentation](https://genome.au.dk/docs/computing-with-mpi/).

## Changes to two-factor authentication

To improve account security, we have tightened two-factor authentication (2FA)
requirements for the open zone.

Previously, users could continue logging in without 2FA after an initial
authentication (with re-authentication with 2FA required after 14 days). Going
forward, all users will be required to complete a 2FA login at least once every
week. This provides additional protection while keeping day-to-day access
convenient.

There are no changes to the closed zones or the GenomeDK Desktop. These services
will continue to require two-factor authentication for every login, as they do
today.

## Ensure correct project ownership

Many GenomeDK projects have existed for years, and in some cases the registered
project owner is no longer actively involved.

As a project owner, you are responsible for ensuring that your projects are 
transferred to another, active GenomeDK user, should it be necessary.

This is especially important since **projects without an active project owner 
are subject to deletion after 6 months**!

If ownership needs to be transferred, contact the GenomeDK support team.
